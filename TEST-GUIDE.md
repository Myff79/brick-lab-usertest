# Brick Lab: test guide

## The run being tested

| Step | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | 15 min (real lab) | Theme, who we are, how the lab works. In a test: read the welcome box. |
| The LEGO task | 15 min, timed | Picture of the pile, ready prompt with a random twist. The AI decides what to build, they follow its recipe. Then "Stop building". |
| Reflection: your prototype | 2 min | Does it make sense? Does it work? What did the AI decide that they wouldn't have? Their lesson. A reality card is made from the answers. |
| Learning loop: develop it, and the concept card | 23 min, free | What they bring with them, then they work freely with their AI and on their own. No ready-made prompt; "Stuck? Get a hint" has four optional prompts. Two checkpoints from the stage (at 8 and 16 minutes in self-paced mode; in moderated mode you send them). Finish with a concept card. Not getting all the way is fine. |
| Reflection | 10 min | Together in the room. In the tool: a few notes, the card, and the last slider. |

## What we want to learn

1. Is it clear what to do, one thing at a time, without a facilitator explaining?
2. Does the time box in the LEGO task work, or do people get stuck on a recipe that doesn't fit their bricks?
3. Does the 2-minute look at the prototype give them an idea to carry on with?
4. Does the loop take them from LEGO to something more real? How many times do they go round?
5. Does everyone end with a concept card, even an unfinished one?
6. Do the twists give different results, or does everyone still end up with benches and tables?

## Before you start

- Don't explain the idea behind the lab. Say only: "This is a lab about building things with your hands and with AI. Think out loud as you go. We're testing the lab, not you."
- Real LEGO: check how they'll get photos from their phone to the laptop.

## What to watch

| Step | Watch for |
|---|---|
| The LEGO task | Do they get the pile picture and prompt into the AI? What does it give back? How do they react when the recipe uses bricks they don't have? Do they stop when told? |
| Look at your prototype | Do they answer quickly? What do they say the AI decided? What lesson do they write? |
| Develop it | How do they start without a ready-made prompt? Do they open the hints, and which one do they use? Do they work on their own too, or only with the AI? How do the checkpoints feel: helpful or controlling? |
| Concept card | Do they find it, and is it easy to fill in? Do they add a picture of their concept (an AI image, a sketch or a screenshot)? |
| Reflection | What do they write, and where do they put the slider? Ask them to say why out loud. |

Also watch: adding pictures (choose a file or drag and drop), the digital table (placing, turning, moving pieces), and "Ask for help".

## After the session (5 to 10 minutes)

Ask the first two before anything else, and don't lead:

1. In your own words, what was this lab about?
2. When did you feel in charge, and when did the AI decide?
3. How far did you get from the LEGO prototype towards something real?
4. Which moment was most confusing? Which was most fun?
5. Would you keep the card? What would you do with it?

Then download the test log and restart the test.
